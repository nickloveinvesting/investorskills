# Rehab Estimation Reference

Comprehensive renovation cost guide for multifamily properties, with specific focus on value-add repositioning of pre-1990s buildings. Cost data reflects DFW metro market conditions for 2024-2025.

---

## Per-Unit Renovation Tiers

### Light Refresh: $3,000 - $8,000/unit

**Scope:**
- Paint: Walls and ceilings (neutral colors)
- Flooring: New carpet OR entry-level LVP
- Fixtures: Replace dated light fixtures and hardware
- Appliances: Clean existing, replace if non-functional
- Cleaning: Professional deep clean

**Best For:**
- Properties with decent bones, just dated finishes
- Quick turnaround needs (3-7 days/unit)
- Tight budgets with modest rent increase targets
- Testing market response before heavier renovation

**Typical Cost Breakdown:**
```
Interior paint (walls/ceilings)     $800 - $1,200
LVP flooring (installed)            $1,200 - $2,500
Light fixtures (5-7 per unit)       $200 - $400
Hardware (hinges, pulls, stops)     $100 - $200
Outlet/switch covers               $50 - $100
Cleaning and make-ready            $300 - $500
Contingency (10%)                  $265 - $490
-----------------------------------------
TOTAL                              $2,915 - $5,390
                                   (Round to $3K-8K with variations)
```

**Expected Rent Premium:** $50 - $150/month

---

### Medium Renovation: $10,000 - $20,000/unit

**Scope:**
- Everything in Light Refresh, plus:
- Flooring: Quality LVP throughout
- Kitchen: Cabinet refacing OR new cabinets, new countertops
- Bathroom: New vanity, toilet, fixtures, possibly tub refinish
- Appliances: New package (stove, refrigerator, dishwasher, microwave)
- Hardware: All new throughout
- Electrical: Update outlets to modern style, add GFI where needed

**Best For:**
- Core value-add plays
- Class C to Class B repositioning
- Properties where competition has renovated
- Targeting 15-25% rent increases

**Typical Cost Breakdown:**

### Heavy Renovation: $25,000 - $40,000/unit

**Scope:**
- Everything in Medium Renovation, plus:
- Kitchen: Full gut with custom layout possible
- Bathroom: Full gut renovation, new tub/shower
- HVAC: New system (mini-split or central)
- Electrical: Panel upgrade, rewire as needed
- Plumbing: Replace supply lines if needed
- Windows: Replace if single-pane
- Washer/dryer connections (if not existing)

**Best For:**
- Deep value-add or opportunistic plays
- Properties with significant deferred maintenance
- 1950s-1970s properties needing systems upgrades
- Major repositioning (Class D to Class B)
- Targeting 30-50%+ rent increases

### Contractor Management

```
Bidding Process:
- Get 3+ bids for each trade
- Specify scope in writing before bidding
- Compare apples to apples (same materials)
- Check references and verify insurance
- Review portfolio of similar work

Payment Structure:
- 10-20% deposit at contract signing
- Progress payments tied to milestones
- 10% retention until final punch list complete
- Never pay ahead of completed work

Key Contract Provisions:
- Fixed price with detailed scope
- Timeline with liquidated damages
- Insurance requirements (GL, WC)
- Warranty provisions
- Change order process
- Cleanup and disposal included
```

## Value-Add ROI Analysis

### Rent Premium Expectations

```
Renovation Level    Investment    Monthly Premium    Annual ROI
----------------------------------------------------------------
Light ($5,000)      $5,000        $75-125           18-30%
Medium ($15,000)    $15,000       $175-250          14-20%
Heavy ($30,000)     $30,000       $350-500          14-20%

ROI Calculation:
Annual ROI = (Monthly Premium × 12) / Investment × 100

Break-even Months = Investment / Monthly Premium

Example:
$15,000 renovation, $200/month premium
Annual ROI = ($200 × 12) / $15,000 = 16%
Break-even = $15,000 / $200 = 75 months (6.25 years)
```

### Prioritization Framework

```
Highest ROI Improvements:
1. Fresh paint (neutral colors): 40-60% ROI
2. New flooring (LVP): 25-35% ROI
3. Updated fixtures/hardware: 30-50% ROI
4. Kitchen countertops: 20-30% ROI
5. Appliance package: 15-25% ROI
6. Bathroom updates: 15-25% ROI

Lower ROI (but may be necessary):
- HVAC replacement: 10-15% ROI
- Window replacement: 8-12% ROI
- Plumbing updates: 8-15% ROI
- Electrical upgrades: 5-10% ROI

Order of Operations:
1. Address health/safety first
2. Curb appeal (first impressions)
3. Kitchens (highest impact)
4. Bathrooms
5. Flooring and paint
6. Systems as needed
```

## Practical Estimating Tips

### Walk-Through Best Practices

1. **Document everything**
   - Photos of every unit, all systems
   - Note conditions systematically
   - Record model numbers and ages

2. **Check behind panels**
   - HVAC access panels
   - Electrical panels
   - Under sinks and in cabinets

3. **Run water at all fixtures**
   - Check pressure and temperature
   - Listen for water hammer
   - Watch drainage speed

4. **Test HVAC in each unit**
   - Note age on data plates
   - Check airflow at vents
   - Listen for unusual sounds

5. **Access attic/crawlspace**
   - Look for moisture/leaks
   - Check insulation condition
   - Inspect structural elements

6. **Review utility records**
   - High usage indicates problems
   - Compare to similar properties
   - Ask for 24-month history

7. **Visit after hours**
   - See actual condition vs. show-ready
   - Check parking utilization
   - Observe tenant behavior

### Bid Package Best Practices

**Always Include:**
- Detailed scope of work per unit type
- Access schedule and coordination requirements
- Material specifications (not just "new flooring")
- Timeline requirements with liquidated damages
- Payment terms tied to completion milestones
- Warranty requirements
- Cleanup and disposal responsibilities
- Insurance and bonding requirements

**Get Multiple Bids For:**
- Any scope over $50,000
- Specialized trades (HVAC, electrical, plumbing)
- Roofing projects
- Exterior/envelope work
- Any structural work

**Evaluate Bids On:**
- Total cost (not just lowest)
- Timeline commitment
- References from similar projects
- Financial stability
- Insurance coverage
- Warranty terms

### Rules of Thumb

1. **Unit interior**: Multiply single unit cost by 1.1-1.2x for common issues
2. **Building systems**: Use average of bids, not lowest
3. **Age factor**: Add 15-25% for buildings over 40 years old
4. **Vacancy during rehab**: Budget 1-2 months rent loss per heavy rehab unit
5. **Scope creep**: Define scope tightly; change orders add 10-20% typically
6. **Material inflation**: Add 3-5% buffer for multi-year projects
