# Pre-1990 Multifamily Due Diligence Reference
## Comprehensive Building Systems Analysis & Capital Planning Guide

**Last Updated: January 2025**
**Target Properties: 1950-1990 Construction**

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Pre-1990 multifamily properties present unique due diligence challenges. Building systems from this era are approaching or exceeding their designed lifespans, creating concentrated capital expenditure requirements. Insurance carriers have tightened underwriting, making certain system deficiencies deal-killers regardless of purchase price.

**Critical Insurance Hard Market Reality (2024-2025):**
- Federal Pacific and Zinsco electrical panels trigger automatic coverage denial
- Aluminum wiring without remediation increasingly causes non-renewals
- Polybutylene plumbing is a known claim risk requiring disclosure
- Environmental hazards (asbestos, lead) compound renovation costs

**Expected CapEx by Decade (Per Unit):**
| Decade | Baseline CapEx | With Major Systems | Insurance Impact |
|--------|---------------|-------------------|------------------|
| 1950s | $25,000-45,000 | +$15,000-25,000 | High scrutiny |
| 1960s | $20,000-40,000 | +$12,000-20,000 | Moderate-High |
| 1970s | $15,000-32,000 | +$10,000-18,000 | Moderate |
| 1980s | $10,000-25,000 | +$8,000-15,000 | Low-Moderate |

## SECTION 1: BUILDING SYSTEMS BY DECADE

### 1.1 1950s Construction (Post-War Era)

**Construction Context:**
Post-WWII housing boom prioritized speed and cost over longevity. Materials were often lower quality due to wartime shortages recovery. Building codes were less stringent than later decades.

#### Plumbing Systems (1950s)
```
Supply Lines:
- MATERIAL: Galvanized steel (standard)
- ORIGINAL LIFESPAN: 40-50 years (all now exceeded)
- STATUS: Critical failure zone - assume full replacement needed
- SIGNS OF FAILURE:
  * Rust-colored water (especially morning first draw)
  * Low water pressure throughout building
  * Visible corrosion at joints and fittings
  * Pinhole leaks (precursor to burst)
  * White/green mineral deposits on exterior

Drain/Waste/Vent (DWV):
- MATERIAL: Cast iron (hub-and-spigot joints)
- ORIGINAL LIFESPAN: 50-75 years
- STATUS: At or beyond design life
- SIGNS OF FAILURE:
  * Slow drains building-wide
  * Sewage odors (failed joints)
  * Wet spots in foundation/crawlspace
  * Visible rust scale at cleanouts

Water Heaters:
- Original units long replaced
- Current units: Verify age (sticker on unit)
- Typical lifespan: 10-15 years
- Budget: $1,200-2,000 per unit (2024-2025)
```

**1950s Plumbing Cost Estimates (2024-2025):**

|-------------|------------|-------|
| Full galvanized repipe (PEX) | $2,500-4,500/unit | Best value option |

| Epoxy pipe lining (drains) | $150-300/linear ft | Alternative to replacement |
| Camera scope inspection | $250-500 | Essential pre-purchase |

#### Electrical Systems (1950s)
```
Panel Type:
- COMMON: Fuse boxes (60-amp service typical)
- STATUS: Obsolete - replacement mandatory for insurance
- ISSUES:
  * Insufficient capacity for modern loads
  * No breaker protection (fuse overfusing common)
  * Two-prong outlets (no grounding)
  * Cloth-insulated wiring (deteriorating)

Wiring:
- MATERIAL: Rubber/cloth insulated copper
- STATUS: Insulation breakdown creates fire risk
- SIGNS OF DETERIORATION:
  * Brittle, cracking insulation
  * Burning smell from outlets
  * Frequent tripped circuits
  * Warm outlets or switches

Service Capacity:
- Original: 60 amps typical
- Modern requirement: 100-200 amps minimum
- HVAC addition requires service upgrade
```

```
Original Systems:
- Gravity furnaces (floor/wall)
- Window AC units or no cooling

Current Reality:
- Original systems replaced multiple times

- Central HVAC rare in original design

Assessment Focus:
- Age of current equipment
- Ductwork presence and condition

#### Structural/Insulation (1950s)
```
Foundation:
- Pier and beam OR slab-on-grade (regional)

### 1.3 1970s Construction

**Construction Context:**
Copper plumbing transition begins. Aluminum wiring at peak usage (1965-1973) due to copper shortage. R-22 HVAC systems become standard. Building envelope improvements begin. Asbestos usage at highest levels.

#### Plumbing Systems (1970s)
```
Supply Lines - MIXED MATERIALS:
- Early 1970s: Galvanized still common
- Mid-1970s: Copper becoming standard
- Late 1970s: CPVC plastic emerging
- CRITICAL: Polybutylene (PB) begins 1978

Material Identification:
- Copper: Orange/reddish, soldered joints
- CPVC: Cream/tan rigid plastic
- Polybutylene: Gray/blue flexible plastic, "PB" stamp
- Galvanized: Silver-gray metal, threaded joints

POLYBUTYLENE RED FLAG (1978-1995):
- CLASS ACTION SETTLEMENTS ($950M+) for failures
- Chlorine in water degrades pipe from inside
- Failures often sudden and catastrophic
- Insurance increasingly requires replacement or excludes water damage
```

|----------|-----------------|----------|
| Galvanized repipe | $2,500-4,500/unit | High - end of life |

| Copper (if failing) | $3,000-6,000/unit | Moderate - inspect first |

#### Electrical Systems (1970s) - CRITICAL ALUMINUM WIRING ERA

```
ALUMINUM WIRING (1965-1973) - FIRE HAZARD
- Approximately 1.5-2 million U.S. homes affected
- CPSC: 55x higher fire risk than copper wiring
- Used due to Vietnam War copper shortage

Visual Identification:
- Silver/gray colored wire at outlets (copper is orange)
- Wire stamped "AL" or "Aluminum" on jacket
- Present at outlets, switches, junction boxes

Failure Mechanism:
- Aluminum expands/contracts more than copper (thermal cycling)
- Repeated cycling loosens connections
- Loose connections create resistance
- Resistance creates heat
- Heat causes aluminum oxide (non-conductive)
- Escalating cycle leads to fire

Insurance Impact:
- Many carriers demand remediation documentation
- Non-renewal notices increasingly common
- Some require COPALUM certification specifically
```

**Aluminum Wiring Remediation Options (2024-2025):**
| Method | Cost Per Connection | Total Per Unit | Notes |
|--------|--------------------|--------------------|-------|
| AlumiConn connectors | $50-75 | $1,500-3,000 | CPSC approved, widely available |
| COPALUM cold-weld | $50-85 | $1,500-3,500 | CPSC preferred, requires certified tech |
| Full copper rewire | $150-250/linear ft | $6,000-15,000 | Eliminates aluminum entirely |

**Remediation Calculation:**
- Average unit: 25-40 outlets/junctions
- AlumiConn: 30 connections x $65 = $1,950/unit
- For 10-unit building: $19,500 total

## SECTION 2: PLUMBING SYSTEMS DEEP DIVE

### 2.1 Galvanized Pipe Analysis

**Life Expectancy by Water Chemistry:**
| Water Type | Expected Life | Your Area Status |
|------------|---------------|------------------|
| Soft water | 50-70 years | Better longevity |
| Hard water | 30-50 years | Accelerated corrosion |
| Aggressive (low pH) | 20-40 years | Worst case |

**Failure Progression:**
1. Interior zinc coating depletes (invisible)
2. Steel begins to rust (pressure drops begin)
3. Rust buildup narrows pipe diameter (flow restricted)
4. Pinhole leaks develop (active failure)
5. Pipe wall weakens (burst imminent)

**Field Assessment:**
- Check water pressure at multiple fixtures
- Run water 2-3 minutes, check for discoloration
- Inspect visible pipes for white/green deposits
- Compare hot water pressure (worse) to cold

**Galvanized Replacement Costs (2024-2025):**

|---------------|-----------|---------------|
| 5 units | $12,500-22,500 | $20,000-40,000 |

| 20 units | $50,000-90,000 | $80,000-160,000 |
| Per unit avg | $2,500-4,500 | $4,000-8,000 |

### 2.3 Cast Iron Sewer Line Assessment

**Camera Scope Report Interpretation:**
| Finding | Severity | Remediation | Cost |
|---------|----------|-------------|------|
| Scaling | Yellow | Monitor | $0 (maintenance) |
| Root intrusion | Yellow | Root treatment | $200-500 |
| Minor cracks | Yellow | Epoxy lining | $100-300/ft |
| Channeling | Yellow-Orange | Epoxy lining | $100-300/ft |
| Offset joints | Yellow | Section repair | $500-2,000 |
| Belly (sag) | RED | Excavation/replacement | $200-400/ft |
| Collapse | RED | Emergency replacement | $250-500/ft |

**Critical Distinction - Channeling vs. Belly:**
- **Channeling**: Water erosion cut groove in pipe bottom. Pipe maintains slope. Trenchless lining CAN fix. Cost: $100-300/ft
- **Belly**: Pipe sags creating low point. Water pools. Slope compromised. Trenchless lining CANNOT fix. Requires excavation. Cost: $200-400/ft + excavation ($3,000-10,000+)

**Sewer Scope Cost:**
| Service | Cost Range | Notes |
|---------|-----------|-------|

| Branch line inspection | $150-300/branch | If main shows issues |
| Locator service | $100-200 | Pinpoints problem areas |
| Second opinion | $250-500 | For Red Light findings |

## SECTION 3: ELECTRICAL SYSTEMS DEEP DIVE

### 3.1 Uninsurable Panel Identification

**Federal Pacific Electric (FPE) Stab-Lok:**
```
VISUAL IDENTIFICATION:
- Panel door: "Federal Pacific" or "FPE" label
- Inside panel: "Stab-Lok" stamped on breakers
- Breakers: Plain handles, orange/tan plunger

WHY UNINSURABLE:
- CPSC testing: 1 in 3 breakers fail to trip
- Estimated 2,800 fires/year attributed to FPE
- $40 million annual property damage
- 25-60% failure rate in overload conditions

INSURANCE ACTION:
- 99% of carriers issue non-renewal
- No approved remediation except replacement
- Budget: $2,200-3,800 per panel
```

**Zinsco/Sylvania Panels:**
```
VISUAL IDENTIFICATION:
- Panel door: "Zinsco" or "Zinsco-Sylvania" or "GTE-Sylvania"
- Breakers: COLORED handles (red, green, blue)
- Bus bars: Aluminum-colored (silver)

WHY UNINSURABLE:
- 32% of tested breakers fail to trip
- 25% show visible damage on inspection
- Breakers can fuse to bus bar
- "Silent failure" - looks normal, fails catastrophically

INSURANCE ACTION:
- Automatic rejection by all major carriers
- Full replacement mandatory
- Budget: $2,200-3,800 per panel
```

**Other High-Risk Panels:**
| Panel Type | Visual Cue | Insurance Status | Replacement Cost |
|------------|-----------|------------------|------------------|
| Challenger | See door label | Varies - often rejected | $2,200-3,800 |
| Pushmatic/Bulldog | Push-button design | High risk | $2,200-3,800 |
| Old fuse boxes | Screw-in fuses | Often rejected | $2,200-3,800 |
| Split-bus panels | No main breaker | Scrutinized | $2,200-3,800 |

### 3.2 Aluminum Wiring Remediation Protocol

**CPSC-Approved Methods:**

**Method 1: AlumiConn Connectors**
- Set-screw copper pigtail attaches to aluminum
- Copper pigtail connects to outlet/switch
- Cost: $50-75 per connection
- Advantages: Widely available, no special certification
- Disadvantages: Aluminum remains in walls
- Insurance acceptance: Generally accepted

**Method 2: COPALUM Cold-Weld**
- Hydraulic tool creates molecular bond
- Permanent copper-to-aluminum connection
- Cost: $50-85 per connection
- Advantages: CPSC top recommendation, most permanent
- Disadvantages: Requires certified technician
- Insurance acceptance: Preferred method

**Method 3: Full Copper Rewire**
- Complete removal of aluminum branch circuits
- All new copper wiring installed
- Cost: $150-250 per linear foot ($6,000-15,000/unit)
- Advantages: Eliminates aluminum entirely
- Disadvantages: Most expensive, most invasive
- Insurance acceptance: Universal acceptance

**Remediation Cost Calculator:**
| Building Size | AlumiConn | COPALUM | Full Rewire |
|---------------|-----------|---------|-------------|
| 5 units | $7,500-12,500 | $8,750-15,000 | $30,000-75,000 |
| 10 units | $15,000-25,000 | $17,500-30,000 | $60,000-150,000 |
| 20 units | $30,000-50,000 | $35,000-60,000 | $120,000-300,000 |

## SECTION 4: HVAC CONSIDERATIONS

### 4.1 R-22 Phase-Out Impact

**Regulatory Timeline:**
- January 1, 2010: No new R-22 equipment manufactured
- January 1, 2020: No new R-22 manufactured (recycled only)
- Current: R-22 supply dwindling, prices escalating

**R-22 System Status:**
- All R-22 systems are 15+ years old minimum
- Most are 25-45 years old
- ANY leak = system replacement (cannot legally recharge with new R-22)
- Recycled R-22 cost: $75-150/lb when available

**R-22 System Identification:**
- Look at data plate on outdoor unit
- "R-22," "HCFC-22," or "Chlorodifluoromethane"
- Units manufactured before 2010

**R-22 Replacement Costs (2024-2025):**
| System Type | Replacement Cost | Notes |
|-------------|-----------------|-------|
| Split system | $5,000-8,000/unit | Most common |
| Package unit | $4,500-7,500/unit | Roof-mounted common |
| Mini-split | $4,000-6,500/unit | No ductwork needed |
| PTAC unit | $1,800-3,000/unit | Direct swap |

### 4.2 System Type Comparison

**Window Unit Properties:**
- Original install: Minimal capital cost
- Tenant pays electric: Lower owner expense
- Limitations: Noise, appearance, limited capacity
- Conversion value: $3,000-5,000 rent premium possible

**PTAC (Packaged Terminal AC) Properties:**
- Common in 1970s-80s multifamily
- Simpler than split systems
- 15-year typical lifespan
- Direct replacement: $1,800-3,000 installed

**Mini-Split Considerations:**
- No ductwork required
- Higher efficiency (SEER 15-25)
- Attractive to tenants
- Installation: $4,000-6,500/unit

**Central HVAC Retrofit:**
- Requires ductwork (often absent in pre-1970)
- Requires electrical capacity
- Highest upfront cost: $7,000-12,000/unit
- Best for extensive renovation projects

### 4.3 HVAC CapEx Timeline

**Equipment Age Assessment:**
| Equipment Age | Status | Action |
|---------------|--------|--------|
| 0-10 years | Good | Monitor, maintain |
| 10-15 years | Fair | Budget for replacement |
| 15-20 years | End of life | Plan replacement |
| 20+ years | Overdue | Replace immediately |

**HVAC Due Diligence Checklist:**
- [ ] Identify all HVAC equipment (manufacturer, model, serial)
- [ ] Decode serial numbers for manufacture date
- [ ] Verify refrigerant type (R-22 = budget replacement)
- [ ] Check maintenance records (if available)
- [ ] Inspect condition (rust, noise, leaks)
- [ ] Test heating and cooling operation
- [ ] Verify electrical capacity for system

## SECTION 5: ENVIRONMENTAL HAZARDS

### 5.1 Lead-Based Paint (Pre-1978)

**Legal Requirements:**
- EPA Lead Disclosure Rule: ALL pre-1978 sales/leases
- Form: "Disclosure of Information on Lead-Based Paint"
- Pamphlet: "Protect Your Family From Lead" (EPA)
- Buyer opportunity: 10-day inspection period

**Testing Options:**
| Method | Cost | Accuracy | Speed |
|--------|------|----------|-------|
| XRF (X-Ray Fluorescence) | $200-400 base + $10-20/test | Highest | Immediate |
| Paint chip lab analysis | $20-40/sample + lab | High | 3-5 days |
| Lead test swabs | $5-15/test | Moderate | Immediate |

**Common Lead Locations (Priority Order):**
1. Window sills and frames (highest concentration)
2. Door frames and trim
3. Baseboards
4. Exterior siding
5. Stair railings
6. Porches and decks

**Management Options:**
| Method | Cost | Duration | Best For |
|--------|------|----------|----------|
| Encapsulation | $6-10/sq ft | 10-20 years | Intact surfaces |
| Enclosure | $8-12/sq ft | Permanent | Large areas |
| Full abatement | $8-15/sq ft | Permanent | Renovation projects |

**Lead Remediation Costs (2024-2025):**
| Scope | Per Unit Cost | Notes |
|-------|--------------|-------|
| Testing only | $300-700 | XRF recommended |
| Minor (trim only) | $1,500-3,500 | Encapsulation |
| Moderate | $3,500-8,000 | Mixed methods |
| Full abatement | $8,000-18,000 | Complete removal |

**EPA RRP Rule (Renovation, Repair, Painting):**
- Applies to ANY pre-1978 residential renovation
- Certified firm required
- Certified renovator on site required
- Lead-safe work practices mandatory
- Fines: Up to $37,500/day per violation

**RRP Triggers:**
- Interior: Disturbing 6+ sq ft painted surface
- Exterior: Disturbing 20+ sq ft painted surface
- Window replacement: ANY size

### 5.2 Asbestos-Containing Materials (ACM)

**Location Heat Map:**
| Material | Location | Likelihood Pre-1980 | Removal Cost |
|----------|----------|---------------------|--------------|
| 9x9 floor tiles | Common areas, units | 99% | $5-15/sq ft |
| Cutback mastic | Under tiles | 90%+ | $10-20/sq ft |
| Popcorn ceilings | Units, common areas | 80%+ | $5-20/sq ft |
| Pipe insulation | Mechanical rooms | 70%+ | $15-50/linear ft |
| Ceiling tiles | Drop ceilings | 60-70% | $8-15/sq ft |
| Drywall compound | Throughout | 30-40% | $5-10/sq ft |
| Vermiculite insulation | Attics | 40% | $11-25/sq ft |
| Cement siding | Exterior | Variable | $10-25/sq ft |

**Visual Identification:**
- **9x9 tiles**: Square vinyl tiles, often light colored
- **Popcorn ceilings**: Bumpy, textured, white/cream
- **Pipe wrap**: White/gray fibrous material on pipes
- **Mastic**: Black, tarry adhesive under tiles

**Testing Requirements:**
| Test Type | Cost | Notes |
|-----------|------|-------|
| AHERA inspection | $500-1,500 | For 20-50 unit property |
| Bulk sampling | $200-500/sample | Lab analysis |
| Air monitoring | $500-2,000 | During abatement |

**Management Options:**
| Method | Cost | Best For |
|--------|------|----------|
| O&M Program | $500-1,000 setup | Non-friable, good condition |
| Encapsulation | $2-6/sq ft | Non-friable, minor disturbance |
| Enclosure | $3-10/sq ft | Areas not being renovated |
| Full removal | $5-20/sq ft | Friable or renovation planned |

**Asbestos Remediation Costs (2024-2025):**
| Material | Removal Cost | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| Popcorn ceiling (200 sq ft room) | $1,100-4,200 | Per room |
| 9x9 floor tiles (1,000 sq ft) | $5,000-15,000 | Includes mastic |
| Pipe insulation | $15-50/linear ft | Mechanical rooms |
| Full building survey | $500-1,500 | Recommended pre-purchase |

### 5.3 Other Environmental Considerations

**Underground Storage Tanks (UST):**
- Common in pre-1970 properties (heating oil)
- Phase I ESA should identify
- Removal cost: $10,000-30,000
- Soil remediation: $50,000-500,000+ if contaminated

**Mold Assessment:**
- Not age-specific but common in older buildings
- Often related to plumbing leaks, roof leaks
- Testing: $300-600
- Remediation: $500-5,000+ depending on scope

**Radon:**
- Geographic dependent
- Testing: $100-300
- Mitigation: $800-2,500 per building

## SECTION 6: CODE COMPLIANCE CONSIDERATIONS

### 6.1 Grandfather Clause Limitations

**General Rule:**
Existing buildings are "grandfathered" under old codes UNTIL:
- Major renovation (typically 50%+ of value)
- Change of occupancy/use
- Specific safety violations identified
- Insurance or lender requirements

**50% Rule (Common Standard):**
If renovation cost exceeds 50% of building value:
- Full current code compliance required
- Fire suppression systems may be triggered
- Accessibility upgrades triggered
- Energy code compliance may apply

**Trigger Events Requiring Upgrades:**
- Electrical panel replacement (may trigger upgrade)
- Roof replacement (may trigger insulation upgrade)
- HVAC replacement (efficiency standards)
- Plumbing repipe (may trigger code updates)
- Any work requiring permit

### 6.2 ADA/Fair Housing Accessibility

**Fair Housing Act Requirements (Post-1991 Construction):**
- Ground floor units in 4+ unit buildings: Accessible
- Accessible routes from parking
- Adaptable features in covered units

**Pre-1991 Buildings:**
- Not required to retrofit proactively
- MUST provide reasonable accommodations when requested
- Tax deductions available for voluntary improvements

**Common Accommodation Requests:**
| Request | Typical Cost | Notes |
|---------|-------------|-------|
| Grab bars | $100-300 | Must allow installation |
| Roll-under sink | $300-500 | Reasonable accommodation |
| Lever door handles | $50-100/door | Low cost compliance |
| Accessible parking | $500-2,000 | Signage and striping |
| Ramp installation | $1,000-5,000 | If requested |

### 6.3 Fire Code Requirements

**Smoke Detector Requirements (Current):**
- Inside each sleeping room
- Outside each sleeping area (hallway)
- Each floor level
- Hardwired with battery backup (most jurisdictions)

**Carbon Monoxide Detector Requirements:**
- If fuel-burning appliances present
- If attached garage
- Near sleeping areas
- May be combined smoke/CO units

**Fire Separation:**
| Requirement | Common Deficiency | Remediation Cost |
|-------------|-------------------|------------------|
| 1-hour unit separation | Hollow-core doors | $400-800/door |
| Self-closing doors | Missing closers | $75-150/door |
| Firestopping | Open penetrations | $50-200/penetration |
| Fire extinguishers | Missing/expired | $50-150/unit |

**Smoke/CO Upgrade Costs (2024-2025):**
| Item | Cost | Notes |
|------|------|-------|
| Battery smoke detector | $15-30 | May not meet code |
| Hardwired smoke detector | $75-150 installed | Most jurisdictions require |
| Combination smoke/CO | $100-200 installed | Recommended |
| Per unit upgrade budget | $200-500 | Including installation |

### 6.4 Egress Requirements

**Bedroom Window Egress:**
- Minimum clear opening: 5.7 sq ft (5.0 at grade)
- Minimum width: 20 inches
- Minimum height: 24 inches
- Maximum sill height: 44 inches

**Common Violations:**
| Issue | Remediation | Cost |
|-------|------------|------|
| Windows too small | Replace window | $400-800 |
| Painted shut | Free window | $50-100 |
| Security bars (no release) | Add quick-release | $50-100 |
| Sill too high | Add platform | $100-200 |
