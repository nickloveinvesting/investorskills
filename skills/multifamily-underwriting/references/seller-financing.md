# Seller Financing Reference

Comprehensive guide to acquiring 5-50 unit multifamily properties using seller financing, owner financing, and creative deal structures. Focused on Texas legal framework and DFW market dynamics.

---

## Why Seller Financing for Small Multifamily

### Advantages Over Traditional Lending

| Factor | Traditional Loan | Seller Financing |
|--------|-----------------|------------------|
| Down Payment | 20-30% | 5-20% (negotiable) |
| Closing Timeline | 45-90 days | 14-30 days |
| Credit Requirements | 680+ FICO | Negotiable |
| DSCR Requirements | 1.20-1.35 | None (seller decides) |
| Prepayment Penalty | 1-5 years | Often none |
| Appraisal Required | Yes | Optional |
| Loan Fees | 1-3% origination | Minimal or none |
| Experience Required | Often 2+ deals | Not required |

### Ideal Candidate Properties

**Best Opportunities:**
- Free-and-clear properties (no existing mortgage)
- Long-term owners (10+ years) with high equity
- Estate/inheritance situations
- Tired landlords seeking passive income
- Properties with deferred maintenance (banks won't lend)
- Below-market rents (banks underwrite to current NOI)
- Owners facing capital gains tax burden

**Property Characteristics:**
- 5-50 units (too small for institutional buyers)
- Pre-1990s construction (banks add reserves)
- Class B/C properties in stable submarkets
- Occupancy 75-90% (value-add opportunity)
- Price range $500K-$5M (sweet spot for individuals)

## Seller Financing Structures

### 1. Seller Carryback (Most Common)

**Structure:**
- Seller holds first-lien note secured by property
- Buyer makes monthly payments to seller
- Title transfers to buyer at closing
- Seller has security interest until paid off

**Typical Terms:**
```
Down Payment:     10-20%
Interest Rate:    5-8% (negotiable)
Amortization:     20-30 years
Term/Balloon:     5-10 years
Monthly Payment:  P&I to seller
```

**Example Deal:**
```
Purchase Price:           $2,000,000
Down Payment (15%):       $300,000
Seller Note:              $1,700,000
Interest Rate:            6.5%
Amortization:             25 years
Term:                     7 years (balloon)

Monthly Payment:          $11,469
Annual Debt Service:      $137,628

Year 7 Balloon Balance:   ~$1,485,000
```

**Pros:**
- Clean structure, easy to understand
- Title in buyer's name (can refinance)
- Seller gets installment sale tax benefits
- Standard foreclosure remedies if default

**Cons:**
- Seller wants reasonable down payment
- Balloon requires refinance or sale
- Due-on-sale if seller has existing mortgage

---

### 2. Contract for Deed (Land Contract)

**Structure:**
- Seller retains title until contract paid in full
- Buyer gets equitable interest and possession
- Payments made per contract terms
- Title transfers upon final payment or refinance

**Texas Specifics (Property Code §5.061-5.085):**
- Must be recorded in county records
- Buyer has 60-day cure period for default
- Seller must provide annual accounting
- Buyer can convert to deed after 48 payments OR 40% paid

**Typical Terms:**
```
Down Payment:     5-15% (lower than carryback)
Interest Rate:    6-9%
Amortization:     20-30 years
Term:             5-10 years
Conversion:       Automatic at 40% equity
```

**Example Deal:**
```
Purchase Price:           $1,500,000
Down Payment (10%):       $150,000
Contract Balance:         $1,350,000
Interest Rate:            7%
Amortization:             25 years
Term:                     7 years

Monthly Payment:          $9,544
40% Equity Threshold:     $600,000 paid
Conversion Point:         ~6 years
```

**Pros:**
- Lower down payment typically accepted
- Seller retains title (security)
- Faster closing (no title transfer)
- Buyer builds equity toward conversion

**Cons:**
- Title not in buyer's name (harder to refinance)
- Texas has strict compliance requirements
- Recording required (not truly "off-record")
- Seller liability continues if underlying mortgage

### 3. Subject-To Existing Financing

**Structure:**
- Buyer takes title "subject to" existing mortgage
- Seller's loan stays in place
- Buyer makes payments on seller's loan
- Seller remains on note (liable if default)

**Critical Considerations:**
- Due-on-sale clause risk (lender can call loan)
- Seller remains liable on original note
- Insurance must cover both parties
- Requires high trust between parties

**When It Works:**
- Seller has low-interest rate locked in
- Loan is assumable or lender is passive
- Seller needs debt relief quickly
- Property underwater or low equity

**Example Structure:**
```
Property Value:           $1,800,000
Existing Mortgage:        $1,200,000 @ 4.5%
Seller's Equity:          $600,000

Structure:
- Take subject-to $1,200,000 first mortgage
- Seller carryback $400,000 second @ 6%
- Buyer down payment: $200,000 (11%)

Monthly Payments:
- First mortgage: $6,080 (to original lender)
- Second (to seller): $2,398
- Total: $8,478
```

**Risk Mitigation:**
- Use land trust to obscure ownership change
- Keep insurance current with lender as mortgagee
- Maintain payment history (don't trigger review)
- Have refinance exit strategy ready

### 4. Wraparound Mortgage (All-Inclusive Trust Deed)

**Structure:**
- New note "wraps around" existing mortgage
- Buyer pays seller on wrap note
- Seller continues paying underlying mortgage
- Spread between rates benefits seller

**Example:**
```
Existing First Mortgage:
- Balance: $800,000
- Rate: 4.5%
- Payment: $4,053/month

Wrap Note to Buyer:
- Amount: $1,400,000 (including equity)
- Rate: 7%
- Payment: $9,319/month

Seller's Monthly Spread:
- Receives: $9,319
- Pays: $4,053
- Keeps: $5,266 (builds equity faster)
```

**Texas Considerations:**
- Legal but complex
- Requires careful documentation
- Insurance and tax escrow management critical
- Default provisions must address both notes

---

### 5. Lease-Option / Lease-Purchase

**Structure:**
- Lease with option to purchase
- Option fee (non-refundable) credited to purchase
- Rent credits may apply to purchase price
- Defined purchase price and timeline

**Best For:**
- Buyers who need time to qualify
- Properties needing operational turnaround
- Testing management capability
- Building track record for financing

**Typical Terms:**
```
Option Fee:               3-5% of purchase price
Option Period:            2-5 years
Rent:                     At or above market
Rent Credit:              25-50% of rent toward purchase
Purchase Price:           Fixed or formula-based
```

**Example:**
```
Purchase Price:           $1,200,000
Option Fee (5%):          $60,000
Monthly Rent:             $10,000
Rent Credit (50%):        $5,000/month

After 3 Years:
- Option fee applied:     $60,000
- Rent credits:           $180,000
- Total credits:          $240,000 (20% effective down payment)
- Balance at exercise:    $960,000
```

## Finding Seller Finance Opportunities

### Seller Profile Identification

**High-Probability Sellers:**

1. **Long-Term Owners (10+ years)**
   - High equity, low basis
   - Facing large capital gains if sold traditionally
   - May prefer installment sale for tax deferral

2. **Estate/Inheritance**
   - Heirs don't want management responsibility
   - Property may have deferred maintenance
   - Motivated to liquidate efficiently

3. **Tired Landlords**
   - Self-managed for years, burned out
   - Health issues limiting management capacity
   - Want passive income without headaches

4. **Pre-Retirement Owners**
   - Need income stream for retirement
   - Don't want lump sum (tax hit)
   - Prefer predictable monthly payments

5. **Owners with Problem Properties**
   - High vacancy, deferred maintenance
   - Banks won't lend on property
   - Need buyer who will take on project

### Sourcing Channels

**Direct-to-Seller:**
- County records research (owners 10+ years)
- Driving for dollars (deferred maintenance)
- Direct mail campaigns (targeted lists)
- Cold calling (requires volume)
- Door knocking (highest conversion)

**Broker Relationships:**
- Explain your creative buying criteria
- Offer to educate on seller financing benefits
- Commission still paid (often from down payment)
- Ask specifically about "seller financing considered"

**Off-Market Networks:**
- Local REIA meetings
- Wholesaler relationships
- Property management companies
- Estate attorneys
- 1031 exchange facilitators (failed exchanges)

## Underwriting Seller-Financed Deals

### Modified Underwriting Framework

Traditional DSCR requirements don't apply, but cash flow analysis remains critical:

**Key Metrics:**

```
Seller Finance DSCR = NOI / Seller Finance Debt Service

Target: 1.10+ (lower acceptable than bank deals)

Cash-on-Cash = Pre-Tax Cash Flow / Total Cash In

Components of "Total Cash In":
- Down payment
- Closing costs (minimal with seller finance)
- Immediate CapEx/repairs
- Working capital reserves
```

### Pro Forma Considerations

**Year 1 Underwriting:**
- Use actual in-place NOI (conservative)
- Seller finance terms as negotiated
- Include reserves for deferred maintenance

**Value-Add Projections:**
- Same rent growth assumptions as traditional
- Expense optimization timeline
- Renovation impact on NOI

**Exit Analysis:**
```
Refinance Scenario:
- Balloon due Year 5
- Target property value at refinance
- Required NOI for 1.25 DSCR with bank
- LTV constraints (75-80%)

Sale Scenario:
- Exit cap rate (typically +25-50 bps)
- Remaining seller note balance
- Net proceeds after payoff
```

## Negotiation Strategies

### Understanding Seller Motivations

**Motivation Discovery Questions:**
1. "How long have you owned the property?"
2. "What would you do with the proceeds if you sold?"
3. "Have you talked to your CPA about the tax implications?"
4. "Are you managing this yourself or using a company?"
5. "What's your timeline for transitioning out?"

**Motivation-Based Positioning:**

| Seller Motivation | Your Pitch |
|-------------------|-----------|
| Capital gains tax fear | Installment sale spreads gains over years |
| Wants retirement income | Predictable monthly payments, secured by real estate |
| Tired of management | You take over all headaches, they get mailbox money |
| Property won't qualify for bank loan | Only creative buyers can close |
| Inherited property | Quick close, no contingencies, fair price |

### Term Negotiation Framework

**Negotiation Variables (Trade-offs):**
```
Higher Down Payment  ←→  Lower Interest Rate
Longer Term          ←→  Higher Interest Rate
Lower Price          ←→  Shorter Amortization
Personal Guarantee   ←→  Better Terms
Larger Balloon       ←→  Lower Monthly Payment
```

**Sample Negotiation Script:**
```
"I understand you're looking for $2 million for the property. 
Based on the current income, here's what I can offer:

Option A: Traditional Terms
- $300,000 down (15%)
- 7% interest
- 25-year amortization
- 7-year balloon

Option B: Higher Down Payment
- $400,000 down (20%)
- 6% interest
- 25-year amortization
- 7-year balloon

Option C: All-Cash Price
- $1,850,000 all cash
- 45-day close

Which structure works better for your situation?"
```

### Overcoming Common Objections

**"I need all cash"**
- "I understand. Most sellers feel that way initially. Can I ask what you plan to do with the proceeds? Many sellers find that an installment sale actually puts more money in their pocket after taxes."

**"My CPA says I should get all cash"**
- "That's interesting. Has your CPA run the numbers on installment sale treatment? I'd be happy to share a comparison showing after-tax proceeds. In many cases, seller financing results in 15-20% more after-tax wealth."

**"What if you don't pay?"**
- "Great question. The note will be secured by the property with a deed of trust. If I default, you foreclose and get the property back, plus you keep all payments I've made. You're actually in a better position than a bank."

**"I want a higher interest rate"**
- "I understand you want a good return. Let me show you how the rate compares to other investments you could make. At 7%, you're getting a secured return that beats most bond funds, and the property is your collateral."

## Texas Legal Framework

### Relevant Statutes

**Property Code Chapter 5, Subchapter D (Executory Contracts):**
- Applies to residential properties (1-4 units and some 5+ units)
- Requires specific disclosures
- 60-day cure period before forfeiture
- Annual accounting requirements
- Conversion rights at 48 payments or 40%

**For 5+ Unit Commercial:**
- Executory contract rules may not apply
- Standard commercial contract law governs
- More flexibility in structuring
- Still requires proper documentation

### Required Disclosures (Residential/Mixed)

Per Texas Property Code §5.069:
- Property condition
- Existing debt on property
- Insurance requirements
- Tax payment responsibility
- Conversion rights

### Dodd-Frank Considerations

**Seller Financing Exemptions:**
- Natural persons (not entities) can seller finance
- 3 properties or fewer per year
- Cannot be construction loans
- Balloon permitted if seller qualifies buyer

**Practical Impact:**
- Most small multifamily seller finance deals qualify for exemption
- Entity sellers may need RMLO involvement
- Document buyer qualification (ability to repay)
- Use qualified attorney for documentation

## Tax Implications

### For Sellers (Installment Sale - IRC §453)

**Benefits:**
- Spread capital gains over payment period
- Defer tax on principal payments received
- Interest income taxed as ordinary income
- Potentially lower marginal tax rates

**Calculation:**
```
Selling Price:                     $2,000,000
Adjusted Basis:                    $800,000
Gain:                              $1,200,000
Gross Profit Ratio:                60%

Year 1 Payment Received:           $437,628
- Principal portion:               $300,000 (down) + ~$28,000 = $328,000
- Interest portion:                $109,628

Taxable Gain (Year 1):             $328,000 × 60% = $196,800
Interest Income:                   $109,628
Depreciation Recapture:            Up to 25% rate (accelerated reporting)
```

**Seller Advantages:**
- Controls timing of gain recognition
- May stay in lower tax bracket
- Interest income provides cash flow
- Secured investment with real estate collateral

### For Buyers

**Deductibility:**
- Interest payments deductible against rental income
- Same depreciation treatment as financed purchase
- Points/origination fees (if any) amortized over loan life

**Basis Considerations:**
- Purchase price establishes basis (same as traditional)
- Improvements added to basis
- Depreciation calculated normally

## Risk Mitigation Strategies

### Buyer Risks and Protections

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| Seller dies, heirs call note | Include "no acceleration on death" clause |
| Seller goes bankrupt | Record deed of trust; your lien survives |
| Seller's existing mortgage called | Get payoff statement; verify with lender |
| Balloon comes due, can't refinance | Negotiate extension provisions upfront |
| Interest rate too high | Build in rate reduction triggers |
| Property underperforms | Structure with lower payments, higher balloon |

**Protective Contract Clauses:**
```
1. No Acceleration Upon Death: "In the event of Seller's death, 
   this Note shall continue according to its terms and shall not 
   be accelerated solely due to Seller's death."

2. Extension Option: "Buyer shall have the right to extend the 
   balloon payment date for two additional 12-month periods upon 
   payment of a 1% extension fee and demonstration of good faith 
   efforts to refinance."

3. Right of First Refusal: "If Seller elects to sell or assign 
   this Note, Buyer shall have the right of first refusal to 
   purchase the Note at the same terms."

4. Prepayment Without Penalty: "Buyer may prepay this Note in 
   whole or in part at any time without premium or penalty."
```

### Seller Risks and Protections

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| Buyer defaults | Deed of Trust allows foreclosure |
| Property damaged | Require adequate insurance, seller as loss payee |
| Buyer doesn't pay taxes | Escrow for taxes OR monitoring rights |
| Environmental issues discovered | Environmental indemnification |
| Buyer strips equity | Assignment of rents clause |

## Exit Strategies

### Refinance (Primary Exit)

**Timeline Planning:**
```
Year 1-2: Stabilize property, improve NOI
Year 3-4: Document improved operations
Year 5: Begin refinance process (6 months before balloon)
Year 6-7: Close refinance, pay seller balloon
```

**Refinance Requirements:**
- Seasoning: 12-24 months ownership
- DSCR: 1.20-1.25 (based on improved NOI)
- LTV: 70-75%
- Documentation: 2 years tax returns, rent roll, T12

### Sale

**When to Sell vs. Refinance:**
- Market cap rates compressed (sell to capture appreciation)
- Better investment opportunities available
- Management challenges not resolved
- Value-add plan fully executed

### Note Extension

**Negotiating Extensions:**
- Begin discussions 12 months before balloon
- Offer extension fee (0.5-1%)
- May negotiate rate adjustment
- Document updated terms in modification agreement

### Assumption

**Selling with Seller Financing in Place:**
- New buyer assumes your note position
- Requires seller approval typically
- You exit, seller continues with new buyer
- May capture equity improvement through sale price

## Third-Party Loan Servicing

### Why Use a Servicer?

Using a third-party loan servicing company adds professionalism and protects both parties:

**Benefits:**
- Payment collection and tracking
- Escrow management (taxes, insurance)
- Annual interest statements (1098/1099-INT)
- Collection calls for late payments
- Payment history documentation
- Neutral third party reduces disputes
- Professional record-keeping for audits

### Texas Loan Servicers

**Recommended Servicers for Seller-Financed Notes:**

| Company | Setup Fee | Monthly Fee | Services |
|---------|-----------|-------------|----------|
| Note Servicing Center | $100-300 | $25-50 | Full service, escrow |
| FCI Lender Services | $150-350 | $30-60 | Nationwide, escrow |
| CRES (Chicago) | $100-250 | $20-45 | Basic servicing |
| Allied Servicing | $200-400 | $35-75 | Full service + collections |

**What Servicers Handle:**
- Payment processing and tracking
- Escrow disbursements (taxes, insurance)
- Late payment notices and collection calls
- Annual statements (tax reporting)
- Payoff calculations
- Subordination requests
- Assumption processing

**Cost Allocation:**
- Typically paid by buyer (add to monthly payment)
- Can be split between parties
- Usually $25-50/month for notes under $500K
- Setup fees often paid at closing

### Self-Servicing Considerations

If not using a third-party servicer:

**Required Tracking:**
- [ ] Payment ledger (date, amount, principal, interest, balance)
- [ ] Annual 1099-INT to borrower
- [ ] Escrow account reconciliation
- [ ] Late fee tracking
- [ ] Payoff statement capability

**Risks of Self-Servicing:**
- Payment disputes without neutral records
- Tax reporting errors
- No professional collection process
- Harder to sell note later (poor documentation)

## TREC Seller Financing Addendum

### Form 26-8 Requirements

In Texas, licensed real estate agents must use the TREC Seller Financing Addendum (Form 26-8) for any seller-financed transaction. Key provisions:

**Section A - Credit Documentation:**
- Buyer must provide credit application info
- Seller can verify employment and credit
- 30-day deadline for seller's credit approval

**Section B - Credit Approval:**
- Seller has _____ days to approve buyer's credit
- Failure to object = approval

**Section C - Promissory Note Terms:**
- Principal amount
- Interest rate (% per annum)
- Prepayment terms (typically without penalty)
- Late fee: 5% of installment if not paid within 10 days
- Default interest: 18% per annum or highest lawful rate

**Section D - Deed of Trust Provisions:**
- Transfer restrictions (due-on-sale clause)
- Insurance requirements (seller as loss payee)
- Tax escrow requirements
- Default and acceleration provisions

**Section E - First Lien Financing (if applicable):**
- Existing mortgage terms if subject-to or wrap
- Payment servicing arrangements

### Key Contract Provisions

**Acceleration Clause:**
```
"If Buyer sells, conveys, leases for more than 3 years, or transfers 
(including a contract for deed), without Seller's prior written consent, 
Seller may declare the balance of the Note immediately due and payable."
```

**Insurance Requirement:**
```
"Buyer shall maintain hazard insurance in an amount sufficient to cover 
any improvements on the Property, with Seller named as mortgagee loss payee."
```

**Tax Escrow Option:**
```
"Buyer shall deposit monthly with Seller the equivalent of 1/12 of the 
estimated annual taxes and insurance premiums."
```

## Note Sale & Secondary Market

### Structuring for Resale

If you may want to sell your seller-financed note later, structure it for maximum marketability:

**Characteristics Note Buyers Prefer:**

| Factor | Preferred | Discount Factor |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| Down Payment | 15-25%+ | <10% = higher discount |
| LTV | 70% or less | >80% = may not buy |
| Interest Rate | 8-10% | <6% = heavy discount |
| Payment History | 12+ months | <6 months = higher risk |
| Seasoning | 12+ payments | New notes = higher discount |
| Credit Score | 650+ | <600 = significant discount |
| Property Type | SFR/Duplex | Large MF = fewer buyers |
| Documentation | Complete | Missing docs = no sale |

- Average seller-financed note balance: $271,655

**Example:**
```
UPB (Unpaid Balance):        $500,000
LTV:                         70%
Seasoning:                   18 months
Payment History:             Perfect

Expected Purchase Price:     85-92% of UPB
Sale Proceeds:               $425,000 - $460,000
```

### Partial Note Sales

Alternative to selling entire note:

**Split Structure:**
- Sell first 60 payments to investor
- Retain remaining payments (balloon or continued stream)
- Get immediate capital while keeping long-term upside

**Example:**
```
Note Terms:                  $400,000 @ 7%, 25-year am, 7-year balloon
Payment:                     $2,827/month

Partial Sale:
- Sell first 48 payments ($135,696 total)
- Investor pays $115,000 today
- You receive payments 49-84 + balloon

Your Yield:                  Still substantial
Investor Yield:              ~12% on capital
```

## Texas Usury Limits

### Maximum Interest Rates

Texas Constitution and Finance Code set usury limits:

**Commercial Loans (including 5+ unit multifamily):**
- No statutory usury limit for commercial loans
- Market rates prevail
- Typical range: 5-10% for seller financing

**Residential (1-4 unit, consumer purpose):**
- Maximum rate: 18% per annum
- Or: Weekly ceiling rate published by Consumer Credit Commissioner
- Typically 8-12% in practice

**Penalties for Usury Violations:**
- Forfeiture of all interest (principal only recoverable)
- Borrower may recover 3x usurious interest paid
- Potential criminal penalties (rare)

### Safe Harbor Practices

**For Commercial Multifamily (5+ units):**
- No usury cap, but market-rate expectations:
  - Current market: 6-9% for seller financing
  - Above 10% may raise borrower resistance
  - Document as arm's-length negotiation

**Best Practice:**
- Reference comparable financing terms in documentation
- Include provision: "Interest rate shall not exceed the maximum lawful rate"

## SAFE Act & Licensing Requirements

### When Licensing is Required

The Secure and Fair Enforcement Act (SAFE Act) requires licensing for certain loan originators:

**Exemptions for Seller Financing:**

**Exempt (no RMLO required) if:**
- Seller is a natural person (not entity)
- Financing 3 or fewer properties per 12 months
- Property will not be borrower's residence (investment only)
- No balloon payment OR seller verified buyer's ability to repay

**License Required if:**
- Entity seller (LLC, Corp) financing residential property
- More than 3 seller-financed transactions per year
- Balloon payment without buyer qualification
- Any involvement by a mortgage broker

### Texas SML Licensing

**If RMLO Required:**
- Apply through NMLS (Nationwide Multistate Licensing System)
- Texas Savings and Mortgage Lending Department oversight
- Background check, education, testing requirements
- Annual renewal and continuing education

**Workarounds:**
- Use RMLO for qualification/disclosure only (~$500)
- Structure as commercial transaction (5+ units = commercial)
- Limit to 3 seller-financed deals per year

## Verifying Free and Clear Status

### Title Verification Process

Before negotiating seller financing, verify seller's equity position:

**Step 1: Preliminary Title Search**
```
Cost: $75-200
Timeline: 3-5 days
Source: Title company or attorney
```

**What to Verify:**
- [ ] Current deed holder matches seller
- [ ] No recorded mortgages or deeds of trust
- [ ] No judgment liens (check seller personally and entity)
- [ ] No federal/state tax liens
- [ ] No mechanic's liens
- [ ] No HOA liens (if applicable)
- [ ] No lis pendens (pending litigation)

**Step 2: Seller Affidavit**
Request seller to sign affidavit confirming:
- Property is free and clear OR disclose all liens
- No undisclosed debts secured by property
- No pending litigation affecting title
- Authority to sell (if entity)

**Step 3: Title Insurance**
- Even with seller financing, get owner's title policy
- Lender's policy if selling note later (required by note buyers)
- Cost: ~0.5-1% of purchase price

### Existing Mortgage Scenarios

If seller has existing mortgage:

**Payoff at Closing:**
- Seller pays off existing loan from proceeds
- Clean title transfers to buyer
- Standard seller-financed structure

**Subject-To:**
- Verify loan balance and terms
- Get payoff statement (valid 30 days)
- Understand due-on-sale clause risk
- Document seller's consent to structure

**Wrap Mortgage:**
- Underlying loan stays in place
- New note "wraps" around existing
- Verify seller has authority to structure
- Confirm servicer can handle wrap payments

## Master Lease Option (MLO) - Advanced Strategy

### What is a Master Lease Option?

A Master Lease Option is a creative financing arrangement where you lease the entire property from the seller with an option to purchase at a predetermined price. You control and operate the property without taking title.

**Key Characteristics:**
- No title transfer at inception
- You pay fixed monthly "rent" to seller
- You keep all cash flow above rent payment
- You have option (not obligation) to purchase
- "Equitable ownership" for lenders and 1031 purposes

### MLO vs Seller Financing Comparison

| Factor | Master Lease Option | Seller Financing |
|--------|---------------------|------------------|
| Title Transfer | At option exercise | At closing |
| Down Payment | $0-5% typically | 10-20% typically |
| Control | Full operational control | Full ownership |
| Cash Flow | Keep difference over rent | Keep all after debt service |
| Lender Recognition | Equitable ownership | Full ownership |
| 1031 Eligible | Yes (equitable ownership) | Yes |
| Exit Options | Buy, extend, 1031, or walk | Refinance, sell, or hold |

### Ideal MLO Scenarios

**Distressed Seller:**
- Tired landlord wants out but doesn't want capital gains hit
- Seller in financial distress, needs immediate relief
- Inherited property, heirs have no interest in managing

**Distressed Property:**
- High vacancy, banks won't lend on current NOI
- Deferred maintenance, property needs stabilization
- Poor records, no clean T12 available

**Distressed Buyer:**
- Limited capital for down payment
- Building track record for future acquisitions
- Need time to improve credit or experience

### MLO Structure Example

```
PROPERTY
24-unit apartment building
Current Value:                    $1,800,000
Current NOI:                      $120,000 (distressed)
Stabilized NOI (projected):       $180,000

MLO TERMS
Master Lease Payment:             $10,000/month ($120,000/year)
Option Price:                     $1,900,000 (locked in)
Option Period:                    5 years
Option Fee:                       $25,000 (credited to purchase)
Extensions:                       Two 1-year extensions available

YOUR CASH FLOW
Year 1 (distressed):
- Gross Rent:                     $14,000/month
- Operating Expenses:             $5,600/month
- Master Lease Payment:           $10,000/month
- Net Cash Flow:                  ($1,600/month) - NEGATIVE

Year 3 (stabilized):
- Gross Rent:                     $20,000/month
- Operating Expenses:             $8,000/month
- Master Lease Payment:           $10,000/month
- Net Cash Flow:                  $2,000/month - POSITIVE

EXIT AT YEAR 5
Property Value (at 6.5% cap):     $2,770,000
Option Price:                     $1,900,000
Your Equity Created:              $870,000
```

### MLO Legal Documentation

**Required Documents:**
1. **Master Lease Agreement** - Lease terms, responsibilities, default provisions
2. **Option Agreement** - Purchase price, exercise terms, extension provisions
3. **Memorandum of Option** - Recorded document protecting your interest
4. **Management Agreement** - Your authority to operate property
5. **Estoppel Certificate** - Seller acknowledgment of terms

**Key Contract Provisions:**

```
Option Exercise: "Lessee may exercise the option to purchase the Property 
at any time during the Option Period by providing written notice to Lessor 
at least 60 days prior to the intended closing date."

Non-Disturbance: "In the event of any default by Lessor on any mortgage 
or lien affecting the Property, Lessor shall take all reasonable steps 
to protect Lessee's rights under this Agreement."

Assignment: "Lessee may assign this Agreement or the Option to any 
entity in which Lessee holds a majority interest without Lessor's consent."
```

### MLO Exit Strategies

**1. Exercise Option - Purchase Property**
- Refinance based on stabilized NOI
- Use new loan to pay option price to seller
- Transition to full ownership

**2. Convert to Seller Financing**
- Renegotiate with seller before option expiration
- Structure as seller carryback with better terms
- Often easier after proving operational capability

**3. Sell/Assign the MLO**
- Assign your rights to another investor
- Capture equity created through stabilization
- Similar to wholesaling, but with operational history

**4. 1031 Exchange**
- MLO qualifies as "equitable ownership"
- Can exchange into larger property
- Must coordinate with qualified intermediary

**5. Walk Away**
- If property doesn't perform as expected
- Lose option fee but limit downside
- No foreclosure, no deficiency liability

## 1031 Exchange Integration with Seller Financing

### Selling WITH Seller Financing (You're the Seller)

When you sell a property and provide seller financing, 1031 exchange complications arise:

**The Challenge:**
- You need full value within 180 days for 1031
- Seller financing means you receive payments over time
- Installment note doesn't satisfy exchange requirements

**Solutions:**

**Option 1: Advance Personal Funds**
```
Sale Price:                       $2,000,000
Seller Financing Note:            $1,700,000
Down Payment Received:            $300,000

1031 Requirement: Invest $2,000,000 within 180 days

Your Action:
- Use $300,000 from down payment
- Add $1,700,000 of personal funds
- Acquire replacement property for $2,000,000
- Receive note payments over time as repayment
```

**Option 2: Note to Qualified Intermediary**
- Structure note so payments go to exchange intermediary
- Intermediary holds funds until replacement identified
- Complex but preserves full deferral

**Option 3: Partial Exchange**
- Accept some installment payments as taxable "boot"
- Exchange down payment portion only
- Pay tax on portion received outside exchange

### Buying WITH Seller Financing (You're the Buyer)

When purchasing replacement property with seller financing:

**Good News:** Seller financing counts as debt for exchange purposes

**Debt Replacement Rules:**
```
Relinquished Property:
- Sale Price:                     $1,500,000
- Debt Paid Off:                  $1,000,000
- Net Equity:                     $500,000

Replacement Property Must:
- Be equal or greater value:      $1,500,000+
- Have equal or greater debt:     $1,000,000+
- Invest all equity:              $500,000

Seller Financing Structure:
- Purchase Price:                 $1,800,000
- Down Payment (your equity):     $500,000
- Seller Note:                    $1,300,000

Result: Full deferral achieved (debt exceeds relinquished)
```

**Combining Bank + Seller Financing:**
```
Purchase Price:                   $2,000,000
Bank First Mortgage (70%):        $1,400,000
Seller Second (15%):              $300,000
Your Down Payment (15%):          $300,000

Total Debt:                       $1,700,000 (counts for exchange)
```

## Land Trust Subject-To Strategy

### Why Use a Land Trust?

For subject-to acquisitions, a land trust can:
- Obscure ownership change from lender
- Avoid triggering due-on-sale clause
- Provide anonymity and asset protection
- Facilitate easier future transfers

### Structure Overview

```
BEFORE: Seller owns property, has mortgage

STEP 1: Seller creates land trust
- Seller transfers property to land trust
- Seller is initial beneficiary
- Trust holds title, beneficiary has control
- Bank typically not notified (no "sale")

STEP 2: Beneficiary interest transfers to you
- Seller assigns beneficiary interest to you
- No deed recorded (trust already owns)
- Mortgage payments continue as normal
- You control property through trust

RESULT: You have equitable ownership
- Make mortgage payments
- Collect rents
- Control property
- No due-on-sale trigger (usually)
```

### Land Trust Documentation

**Required Documents:**
1. **Trust Agreement** - Creates the land trust, names trustee
2. **Warranty Deed to Trust** - Transfers property to trust
3. **Beneficiary Assignment** - Transfers beneficial interest to you
4. **Management Agreement** - Your authority to operate
5. **Power of Attorney** - If needed for trust actions

### Risk Mitigation

**Due-on-Sale Clause Risk:**
- Garn-St. Germain Act protects some transfers
- Land trust to same beneficiary generally safe
- Beneficiary change is the gray area
- Keep payments current to avoid lender attention

**Best Practices:**
- Use experienced real estate attorney
- Maintain all insurance requirements
- Keep property taxes current
- Never miss a mortgage payment
- Have refinance exit plan ready

## Quick Reference: Seller Financing Deal Analysis

### 30-Second Screening

Ask these 5 questions before deep analysis:

1. **Is property free and clear?** (Simplest structure)
2. **What's seller's motivation?** (Taxes? Retirement? Exit?)
3. **What's current DSCR potential?** (Can it service debt?)
4. **What's your total cash-to-close?** (Down + closing + CapEx)
5. **What's your refinance path?** (How do you exit?)

### Seller Financing Underwriting Multipliers

Quick adjustments vs traditional deals:

| Factor | Adjustment |
|--------|------------|
| Lower down payment | +10-15% cash-on-cash vs bank deal |
| Interest rate premium | +1-2% over bank rates |
| Shorter amortization | Plan for higher payments |
| Balloon risk | Factor extension or refi costs |
| Closing costs | Usually 50% less than bank |
| Speed to close | 2-4 weeks vs 45-90 days |

### Red Flags in Seller Financing

**Walk Away If:**
- Seller won't provide title commitment
- Existing mortgage has aggressive due-on-sale language
- Seller is in active litigation or bankruptcy
- Property has unresolved environmental issues
- Seller wants above-market interest rate (>10%)
- No clear path to refinance at balloon
- Combined leverage exceeds 90% LTV

### Go/No-Go for Seller Financing

**GREEN - Proceed Confidently:**
- Free-and-clear property
- Seller motivated by tax deferral
- Terms at or below market rates
- Clear refinance path within term
- Positive cash flow from Day 1

**YELLOW - Proceed with Caution:**
- Existing mortgage (subject-to risk)
- Higher than market interest rate
- Short balloon (3-5 years)
- Deferred maintenance requiring CapEx
- Occupancy below 85%

**RED - Significant Risk:**
- Seller in financial distress
- Multiple lien holders
- Due-on-sale likely to trigger
- No seasoning before balloon
- DSCR below 1.0 at acquisition
