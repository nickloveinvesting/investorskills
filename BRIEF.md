# BRIEF — InvestorSkills

**Status:** Self-authored under explicit creative delegation. The four structural
decisions below were chosen by Nick; the rest is authored and labeled as such.

---

## 1. Vibe, in three to five words

Forensic. Expensive. Quietly certain.

References, none of them websites:
- A Bloomberg terminal at 4am — dense, monospaced, indifferent to whether you like it
- An architect's blueprint elevation — line weight as hierarchy, no decoration that isn't load-bearing
- The weekend edition of a broadsheet financial paper — serif authority, long measure, white space as confidence

## 2. The scroll journey, section by section

1. **Recognition** — the reader arrives inside a drafting plane. The claim is about refusal, not features.
2. **The cost** — four lines that advance while the frame holds. What generic AI actually costs on a deal.
3. **The mechanism** — the only act that reads like a document. Prompt versus skill, side by side.
4. **The refusal** — the peak. The product demonstrates itself by declining to answer.
5. **The range** — the catalog travels laterally. Nine categories, forty-six skills.
6. **The arithmetic** — real numbers land. Price ladder against what the alternative costs.
7. **Commitment** — one action, footer inside the frame, no dead tail.

## 3. The energy curve

Quiet at the open. Rising through act 2. A hard mechanical spike at act 4 — the loudest
thing on the page is a machine saying no. Drops to calm through 5 and 6, because the range
and the price should not shout. Decisive, not loud, at the close.

## 4. Feeling curve, stage by stage

| Act | Emotion | What on screen causes it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Recognition | Recognised, slightly caught out | The claim is about the reader's own shortcut, not the product |
| 2 The cost | Discomfort, sharpening | Lines replacing each other in a held frame; nowhere to look away |
| 3 The mechanism | Relief, orientation | Two columns, plain type, the first thing on the page that behaves like a document |
| 4 The refusal | Surprise, then respect | A live panel refuses to produce a number and says exactly why |
| 5 The range | Steadying breadth | Categories moving sideways under a still headline |
| 6 The arithmetic | Cold clarity | Counters resolving on real figures, no exclamation |
| 7 Commitment | Decided | One control, magnetised, nothing after it |

**The peak:** act 4. The moment the visitor tries to get an answer out of it and it
refuses. Written as the sentence they would say to someone else:

> "It wouldn't give me a number until I told it the ARV. That's the whole pitch."

**The tell-someone sentence:** *It's the site where the product refuses to answer you,
and that's the demo.*

## 5. One thing no site they've seen does

Sell a thing by having it decline to work. Every AI product page shows output. This one
shows a refusal, live, driven by the visitor's own pointer — because refusal is the actual
differentiator between a workflow and a prompt, and describing it in a bullet is worth
nothing.

## 6. Distance from premium-minimal

**Editorial.** Type-led, long measure, serif display against a monospaced data voice.
Structure carries the weight; motion is restrained and never decorative. Not minimal
(there is real density), not maximalist (nothing is loud for its own sake).

## 7. One unbroken world, or distinct scenes

**Distinct scenes.** Seven acts, each behaving differently. No continuous camera flight.
Per the skill's own guidance, an unbroken chain is the most fragile build available and
exists mainly to hide cuts — varying the device removes the need.

## 8. Assets

**None supplied. Fully generated, zero raster.** Every visual is inline SVG, CSS gradient,
or type. No image files, no video, no external requests except two font families.

This is a deliberate constraint, not a limitation to hide: the subject is drafting,
measurement, and arithmetic, so a vector drafting language is the honest world for it.
Photoreal compositing would have been the wrong register for a page about underwriting
discipline.

## Act score

| # | Act | Device family | Span | Why this device here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recognition | `parallax` + `kinetic` | 2.6 | Depth planes establish the drafting world before a word is read |
| 2 | The cost | `pin` | 3.2 | Held frame, advancing cues — the reader cannot escape the argument |
| 3 | The mechanism | `flow` + `reveal` | — | Document behaviour, deliberately the flattest act on the page |
| 4 | The refusal | `pin` + pointer | 2.4 | Interaction, not scroll, carries the peak |
| 5 | The range | `pan` | 3.4 | Lateral travel reads as breadth without a wall of cards |
| 6 | The arithmetic | `flow` + `count` | — | Numbers resolving under the reader's scroll, calm |
| 7 | Commitment | `pin` + magnet | 1.15 | Short, final, footer inside the stage |

Families used: parallax, kinetic, pin, flow, reveal, pan, count, pointer. Eight, against a
minimum of four. No family repeats in adjacent acts. No `scrub` act, because there is no
footage and a generated video would have been the diorama failure the skill warns about.

**Authored silence:** act 3 has no motion beyond a single reveal, and act 6 has none beyond
the counters. Both are intentional rests between the spike at 4 and the close at 7. A
verification pass should read them as designed quiet, not dead scroll.
